# eViral — Business Overview

> **Product:** eViral, an Orbyte module for AI-assisted social media marketing
> **Tagline:** *Tell us what you're selling. We'll market it. You approve.*
> **Event:** Orbyte Smart SME Challenge: Less Busywork, Better Business (Domain 2), sponsored by Alpha Red Solutions Sdn Bhd
> **Main SDG targets:** 8.2 (productivity through technological upgrading and innovation) and 8.3 (support for entrepreneurship and growth of MSMEs). Possible link to SDG 10.

**On the name:** it follows Orbyte's "e + task" pattern (eLeave, eClaim, eSign). One caution: "viral" is a promise nobody can keep. In marketing copy, say eViral helps you *show up consistently*, not that it makes posts go viral. The product's own guardrails block "guaranteed viral" style claims in generated posts.

---

## 1. One-paragraph summary

eViral lets a small business market a product without a marketing team. The owner enters **a product name and a photo** (price and a short note are optional), picks a **posting schedule**, and eViral builds the campaign: improved product images, a short promo video, platform-specific captions for Facebook, Instagram and TikTok, and a calendar of posts. AI does the creative work; rules check every fact against what the owner entered; an authorised person **approves** the campaign; then posts go out automatically on schedule. Every step is recorded in an activity log, and the owner can pause or cancel at any time.

## 2. The problem

### 2.1 The pain point (specific, not "SMEs are inefficient")

Malaysian micro and small business owners know they need to be visible on social media, but they don't have the time, design skills or budget to produce content consistently. A typical launch looks like this: the owner snaps a photo on their phone, spends an evening writing captions and fiddling with a design app, posts once, then goes quiet for weeks. Prices and promotion dates get typed by hand, and sometimes they're wrong.

### 2.2 Who feels it

| Persona | Situation | Pain |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner-operator** (home baker, boutique, café, online seller) | Runs everything, posts from their own phone | No time; inconsistent posting; no design skills |
| **Small team with one "social media person"** | A staff member handles posts on top of other duties | Needs the owner's sign-off by WhatsApp; approvals get lost; no record |
| **Owner with lower digital or English confidence** | Prefers BM or mixed BM/EN | Hard to write polished captions; tools are English-first |

### 2.3 Why existing options fall short

| Option | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Schedulers (Hootsuite, Buffer, Meta Business Suite) | Schedule content but **do not create it**. The owner still writes every caption and makes every image or video. |
| Generic AI chatbots | Write captions but can't see your product photo, can't schedule, have no approval roles and no audit trail. |
| Design and video tools | Help with visuals but need effort and skill on every post. |
| Hiring a social media manager or agency | Estimated at RM3,000–8,000 per month (team estimate, to be verified). Out of reach for most micro businesses. |

**The gap eViral fills:** one input (a product and a photo) becomes a complete, scheduled campaign, with facts checked and a person in control.

## 4. The solution

### 4.1 Core promise

> "Give us a product name and a photo. We'll make the content and the schedule. You approve, and eViral posts for you."

"Autopilot" describes the effort saved. It must never mean publishing without a human. A person always approves, and can pause at any time.

### 4.2 Primary use case: launch a product in three inputs

1. **Product name** (for example, "Kek Lapis Sarawak Premium")
2. **Product photo** (a normal phone photo)
3. **Schedule** (a launch sequence, a weekly cadence, or custom days and times)

Optional: price, a short note ("limited batch", "ready 1 Dec"), preferred language, goal ("get pre-orders").

eViral then:
- **Understands the product** from the photo and name, and shows what it "sees" for the owner to confirm (for example "round layered cake, brown, sliced").
- **Improves the visuals**: cleans up the photo, replaces the background, and makes a short promo video from the image.
- **Writes the posts** per platform and per stage of the campaign, in the owner's language and tone.
- **Plans the schedule** and fills the calendar.
- **Checks the facts**, then asks the Owner to approve the whole campaign in one review.
- **Posts automatically** at the scheduled times after approval, and logs everything.

### 4.3 It is not limited to new products

| Use case | Trigger | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **New product launch** (primary, demoed) | Owner adds product and photo | Teaser, launch, reminder, last chance |
| Promotion or sale | Owner adds a discount and dates | "20% off until 30 Nov" |
| Restock or low stock | Stock status changes (later: POS event via Orbyte Connect) | "Back in stock" |
| Event or seasonal campaign | Calendar date | Raya, CNY, Merdeka, 11.11 |
| Evergreen content | Weekly schedule | Product spotlight, behind the scenes, customer tips |
| Hiring or announcements | Other Orbyte module (later) | "We're hiring" from Orbyte Hiring |

The engine is the same for all of them: **trigger → facts → creative → check → approve → schedule → publish → log.**

### 4.4 Scheduling and automation

| Mode | What it does | Human control |
|---|---|---|
| **Launch sequence** | A template expands into a run of posts, for example teaser (day 0), launch (day 3), reminder (day 5), last chance (day 7) | Owner approves the whole sequence once. Posts then go out automatically. |
| **Recurring cadence** | "Tue / Thu / Sat at 8 pm", with new drafts prepared ahead of time for each slot | Each batch of drafts waits for approval before publishing. Nothing publishes unapproved. |
| **Custom** | Owner picks dates and times per post | Same approval rule |
| **Controls** | Pause, resume or cancel a campaign; edit a post (which sends it back for approval) | Owner role |

### 4.5 The core automated journey (maps to the brief's five steps)

| Step | Rubric term | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Trigger** | New product submitted, promotion added, recurring schedule fires, or (later) an Orbyte event |
| 2 | **Connected data** | Business profile, product facts the owner entered, product photo, past performance, and (designed for) Orbyte Connect product and POS data |
| 3 | **Decision logic** | **Rules:** campaign plan, times, platform formats, fact validation. **AI:** product understanding, copywriting, image and video creation |
| 4 | **Human approval** | Owner reviews the campaign (images, video, captions, schedule, warnings) and approves, edits or rejects. Staff can prepare but cannot approve. |
| 5 | **Visible outcome** | Posts publish on schedule, appear in the calendar and activity log, and feed analytics |

### 4.6 Features by priority

| Priority | Feature |
|---|---|
| **MVP (must work live)** | Add product with name and photo; AI image improvement; captions per platform; launch-sequence schedule; fact validator; Owner approval; scheduled auto-publishing (mock publisher); activity log; role switcher |
| MVP (if time) | Short promo video from the product image; recurring weekly cadence; simulated analytics; BM / EN / mixed language |
| Stretch | Comment reply drafts with escalation to a human |
| Roadmap | Real publishing to Meta and TikTok; POS and e-commerce triggers via Orbyte Connect; trend insights from permitted data; multi-outlet |

### 4.7 What makes it more than "a chatbot that writes captions"

1. **Starts from your product photo**, not a blank prompt.
2. **Creative plus checking:** AI makes the content; rules verify facts before any human sees it.
3. **Whole campaign, not one post:** schedule, sequence and calendar.
4. **Control built in:** roles, approval, pause, audit trail.
5. **Product stays recognisable:** the product's own photo is the starting point for visuals, so the AI improves the setting and quality instead of inventing a different product. Original and AI-improved versions are shown side by side and labelled.
6. **Orbyte-native:** shared roles and audit, pay per approved action.

## 7. Business model

Aligned with Orbyte's usage-based approach. Costs differ by content type (images and video use paid AI credits), so pricing should reflect that.

| Tier | What's included | Price idea (placeholder; validate) |
|---|---|---|
| **Free block** | A small monthly allowance of text drafts and a few images | RM0 |
| **Pay per approved post** | Text and image post | Low per-post fee |
| **Video post** | Post with AI-generated short video | Higher per-post fee (video costs more to generate) |
| **Add-ons (later)** | Comment-reply drafts, analytics reports, multi-outlet | Per use or small bundle |
| **Partner / revenue share (later)** | POS and e-commerce partners embedding eViral through the adapter | Referral or API fee |

Work out real unit costs with the AI providers' pricing calculators before fixing prices. **Why per approved post:** cost follows value, quiet months are free, and it mirrors eClaim and eSign.

## 11. Validation plan (optional but valuable)

Aim for **3–5 conversations** with real SME owners or staff (a home baker, a boutique, a pasar malam seller).

**Ask:**
1. How often do you post, and how long does a launch post take you?
2. Who writes the captions and makes the photos and videos? Who approves?
3. Have you ever posted the wrong price or date? What happened?
4. Would you trust AI to write and design posts? What would worry you?
5. Would you prefer to approve on WhatsApp, a phone, or a website?

**Show them** the prototype for 2 minutes with no explanation and note where they hesitate. That feeds your "easy to learn" claim. Add one slide: "What we heard from N owners."

## 12. Privacy and PDPA (short statement for the proposal)

The prototype uses **only synthetic data**. In a real deployment eViral would:

- Collect only business-side data (products, photos, schedules, brand settings) and, for the stretch feature, public comment text.
- Avoid putting customer personal data in AI prompts or logs; strip commenter names before sending text to an AI provider.
- Warn owners not to upload photos of identifiable people without their consent.
- Be transparent that images and text are processed by third-party AI providers, and review where data is processed and stored, given PDPA's cross-border transfer rules.
- Use encryption in transit and at rest, role-based access and audit logs (Security principle).
- Apply retention limits and allow deletion (Retention principle).
- Keep a human in the loop for anything customer-facing (publishing and replies).

*(Check the principle names against the current PDPA text before submission. This is not legal advice.)*

## 13. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation / fallback |
|---|---|---|
| **AI writes a wrong or misleading fact** (hardest risk) | Wrong or misleading post | Facts come only from what the owner entered; validator blocks mismatches; AI-observed product attributes need owner confirmation |
| **AI-improved image misrepresents the product** | Customers feel misled | Start from the real photo; show original and improved side by side; owner must confirm "this still looks like my product"; label AI-generated media |
| Platform publishing needs app review | Cannot publish live in time | Publisher adapter with a mock implementation; real Meta adapter on the roadmap; "copy and download" option |
| AI provider outage, slow jobs or exhausted credits during the demo | Demo fails | Pre-generated demo assets; fall back to the original image with a text overlay; cache AI outputs |
| Over-promising ("go viral") | Reputational harm | Banned-claims list; honest marketing language |
| Platform policy or terms changes | Integration breaks | Adapter pattern isolates platform code |

## 15. Pitch outline (about 3 minutes)

1. **Hook (15s):** a real story from your interviews, or: "A home baker launches a new cake. It takes her three evenings, and she still posts the wrong price."
2. **Problem (30s):** inconsistent posting, no design skills, manual copying; one or two verified statistics.
3. **Solution (30s):** eViral, an Orbyte module: a name, a photo and a schedule in; a campaign out.
4. **Live demo (75s):** add product, watch images, captions and schedule appear, a bad price blocked, Staff can't approve, Owner approves, calendar fills, post publishes, activity log.
5. **Benefit (20s):** minutes per campaign, errors blocked, first-time use without training.
6. **Orbyte fit and model (20s):** adapters, shared roles and audit, pay per approved post.
7. **Risk and roadmap (10s):** hardest risk is accuracy and product fidelity, handled by validation and side-by-side review; roadmap is live publishing and POS triggers.
